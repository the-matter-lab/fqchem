# Complexity and limits

The results below specify how the algorithm accesses its input. A query to an individual potential term, a phase flip on a hidden subspace, and a classical description of a local Hamiltonian are different resources.

## State preparation and simulation

| Question | Formal result |
| --- | --- |
| What is the best success probability for one hidden marked item? | Up to the first maximum, Grover attains the exact upper bound sin²((2T+1) arcsin(1/√N)) for T queries. Near-certain success requires (π/4−o(1))√N queries. [Lean proof](SearchOptimality.lean) · [Zalka](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.60.2746) |
| Can every two-sparse Hamiltonian be simulated faster than its scaled evolution time? | The parity-encoded Hamiltonian forces at least N/4 whole-row queries at trace distance 1/4 and scaled time πN/2. [Lean proof](SparseNoFastForwarding.lean) · [Berry et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-006-0150-x) |
| How many oracle calls does preparing an unknown ground state require? | For the complementary projectors of N orthonormal candidates, uniform energy error ε implies N√(1−ε) ≤ (2T+1)√N after T phase queries. The proof allows arbitrary intervening unitaries. [GroundStateSearch](GroundStateSearch.lean#L154) · [Bennett et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9701001) |
| Can a simulator skip the evolution time when it queries individual binary potential terms? | A two-orbital Hamiltonian with onsite energy in [0,π] encodes the parity of N terms in its antibonding population at time N. Any circuit reproducing that probability on every input to error strictly below 1/2 needs at least N/2 term queries. [OrbitalSimulation](OrbitalSimulation.lean#L113) · [Beals et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9802049) |
| Which input bits materially affect an orbital measurement? | A T-query Born probability has a real polynomial representative of degree at most 2T. If its variance on uniform independent bits is at least ε>0, some bit has mean squared flip effect at least (ε/(2T))⁷. An explicit-constant bound applies to populations after binary onsite potential pulses. A bonding/antibonding experiment realizes variance 1/4. [Query polynomial](AaronsonAmbainisQuery.lean) · [Onsite evolution](AaronsonAmbainisOrbitals.lean) · [Influence theorems](AaronsonAmbainis.lean) · [Pinned Aaronson–Ambainis proof](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis/tree/2a6ceaef872ae993708950f951989134084b851a) |

For fast-forwarding, the concatenation lemma bounds the error and gate count of repeated simulator calls. The two-sparse path construction proves that the far endpoint can be reached only on the track selected by the input parity. The sparse row-oracle theorem gives an N/4 query lower bound. [Concatenation proof](FastForwarding.lean) · [Crossed paths](SparseParityPath.lean) · [Atia and Aharonov (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01637-7) · [Berry et al. proof](https://fqchem.matterlab.ca/#paper/berry-sparse-no-fast-forwarding)

The two-orbital simulation lower bound uses parity orthogonality and the polynomial degree bound. The influence theorem answers a different question about variation over the input ensemble. The [complete dependency graph](../../docs/theorems/README.md) shows the proof branches.

For Monte Carlo estimators, a signed-path example proves a sampling lower bound as a function of mean sign and target accuracy. A separate proof compiles 3-SAT into a cubic spin lattice and bounds the decision error from one signed energy estimate. [Thermal decision reduction](../StatePrep/ImaginaryTimeProjection/SpinGlassLatticeDecision.lean) [Sign problem](../StatePrep/ImaginaryTimeProjection/SignProblem.lean)

Simon’s unary language has polynomial quantum query and decoding cost relative to a single oracle. Every clocked probabilistic oracle Turing machine fails on arbitrarily large unary inputs. [Lean proof](../../integrations/qatlib/FQChemQatlib/Complexity/SimonTuringSeparation.lean)

The Shor integration connects an executable Euclidean decoder to Fourier sampling and bounds the logical gate count of the same compiled circuit. The classical reduction proves the success fraction for arbitrary odd composite moduli, including those with more than two distinct prime factors. [Order finding](../../integrations/forshor/FQChemForShor/OrderFinding.lean) · [Factoring reduction](FactoringReduction.lean)

## Ground energy and verifier circuits

- **Kitaev history Hamiltonian.** An amplified verifier circuit maps to a serialized five-local Hamiltonian with disjoint YES and NO ground-energy thresholds, an inverse-polynomial promise gap and a polynomial-size description. An accurate preparer and energy measurement suffice to decide this promise. For ordinary decision circuits, explicit majority amplification takes acceptance ≥2/3 or ≤1/3 to the required history-state promise with polynomial growth. The integration documents the precise circuit promise, resource counts and scope of the formal complexity result. [Bounded-error proof](../../integrations/qatlib/FQChemKitaev/BoundedErrorReduction.lean) · [Lean 4.35 package](../../integrations/qatlib/) · [Kitaev, Shen and Vyalyi](https://doi.org/10.1090/gsm/047)
- **Fixed-particle encoding.** A sum of local Hermitian qubit matrices expands into Pauli words. A dual-rail construction on 2n orbital modes, with n electrons and a penalty for invalid pair occupations, preserves the normalized low/high energy promise. The penalty bound covers superpositions with leakage. Two-local input gives bilinears and products of bilinears, plus the onsite number penalty. [Pauli expansion and reduction](ElectronicStructurePauli.lean) · [Fermionic operators and VQE threshold](ElectronicStructureOperators.lean) · [Liu, Christandl and Verstraete](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.110503)
- **Circuit to fixed-particle energy.** The checked integration decodes the verifier’s local matrices, proves equality with the history Hamiltonian, and applies the fixed-particle reduction. It preserves the original energy thresholds, including the lower bound for every normalized state in the fixed-number space. A clocked uniform generator can supply the source circuit, with X gates loading the actual classical basis input. [Circuit-to-fermion proof](../../integrations/qatlib/FQChemKitaev/ElectronicStructure.lean) · [Uniform generator and input preparation](../../integrations/qatlib/FQChemKitaev/UniformCircuits.lean)
- **Fermionic two-particle densities.** The representable marginals form a nested family of convex sets. A two-body Hamiltonian has exactly the same range of energies on the full N-fermion states as its linear energy functional on their representable marginals. [Lean proof](FermionicMarginals.lean)
- **Physical electronic integrals.** O’Gorman, Irani, Whitfield and Fefferman prove QMA-completeness for electronic structure with specified orbitals and a fixed electron number. The library evaluates the Gaussian overlap and on-site Coulomb integral in three dimensions, matches hopping parameters, and proves an independent-set energy gap for an explicit basis of localized orbitals. [Gaussian Coulomb integral](FixedBasisCoulomb.lean) · [Physical Slater energy gap](SlaterEnergyHamiltonian.lean) · [Published theorem](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.020322)

Kitaev's class-level result is QMA-completeness of the worst-case local **energy decision problem**. It implies BQP-hardness since a BQP verifier can ignore its witness. Complexity is a statement about a family of inputs, a promise gap and a reduction. [Kempe, Kitaev and Regev](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539704445226)
